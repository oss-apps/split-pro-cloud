Splitting rent sounds simple until one bedroom is clearly bigger, has its own bathroom, or one roommate earns twice as much as the others. Most rent arguments are not really about money. They are about a split that felt unfair to someone from day one and never got talked about.

The good news: there are only three common ways to split rent, and each one is easy to calculate. The hard part is agreeing, and that is easier when everyone can see the numbers side by side.

Throughout this guide we'll use one example flat: **$3,200 a month**, three bedrooms of **180, 140 and 110 sq ft**, shared by Alex, Priya and Jordan.

## Option 1: Split equally

Everyone pays the same: $3,200 ÷ 3 = **$1,066.67** each (one person pays a cent less so it adds up).

**Works well when** the bedrooms are roughly the same size and nobody has a private bathroom or balcony.

**Feels unfair when** one room is noticeably better. Jordan, in the smallest room, pays the same as Alex in a room 60% bigger.

## Option 2: Split by room size

The simplest version divides the whole rent in proportion to each bedroom's size. The three rooms add up to 430 sq ft, so Alex's 180 sq ft is 41.9% of the private space.

|        | Room      | Share | Pays      |
| ------ | --------- | ----- | --------- |
| Alex   | 180 sq ft | 41.9% | $1,339.54 |
| Priya  | 140 sq ft | 32.6% | $1,041.86 |
| Jordan | 110 sq ft | 25.6% | $818.60   |

This is fairer to Jordan, but it ignores something important: **everyone uses the kitchen, living room and bathroom equally**. A big part of what you pay for is shared space.

### The better version: split shared space equally

So most people split the rent in two parts:

1. A part for **shared space**, split equally.
2. The rest, split **by bedroom size**.

If you say 30% of the rent pays for shared space, that is $960, or $320 each. The other $2,240 is split by room size:

|        | Shared part | Room part | Pays          |
| ------ | ----------- | --------- | ------------- |
| Alex   | $320.00     | $937.68   | **$1,257.68** |
| Priya  | $320.00     | $729.30   | **$1,049.30** |
| Jordan | $320.00     | $573.02   | **$893.02**   |

How much counts as "shared"? There is no rule. A rough guide is the share of the floor plan that isn't bedrooms. If you don't have a floor plan, 25–40% is a reasonable starting point. Try a few values together and pick one everyone is comfortable with.

**Adjust for extras.** A private bathroom, a balcony or much better light are worth something. The easiest way to account for them is to add some square feet to that room's size, say 20–40 sq ft for an en suite, rather than inventing a separate formula.

## Option 3: Split by income

Everyone pays the same share of what they earn, instead of the same amount. If Alex earns $85,000, Priya $62,000 and Jordan $54,000 a year, the total is $201,000, and Alex's share is 42.3%.

|        | Income  | Share | Pays      |
| ------ | ------- | ----- | --------- |
| Alex   | $85,000 | 42.3% | $1,353.23 |
| Priya  | $62,000 | 30.8% | $987.07   |
| Jordan | $54,000 | 26.9% | $859.70   |

**Works well for** couples, and close friends who want everyone to have about the same money left over each month.

**Needs care because** it asks people to share their income, and it can feel odd if the person paying the most has the smallest room. Some households combine methods: split by room size, then let the higher earner pick the best room.

## Couples sharing a room

A couple in one bedroom uses more of the shared space than one person does. Two common ways to handle it:

- **Treat the couple as one row** with their room size, and let them split that amount between themselves. Simple, and slightly generous to the couple.
- **Treat them as two people**, each with half the room's size. They each pay a full share of the shared space, which is usually seen as fairer when the flat is otherwise shared by singles.

## How to actually agree

1. **Do it before anyone picks a room.** Once someone has moved their bed in, every number feels personal.
2. **Put the options side by side.** Work out each option with your real numbers, using the examples above, and write down how much each person pays compared to an equal split.
3. **Share the link** in your group chat, so everyone looks at the same numbers.
4. **Write it down.** One message with the agreed amounts saves an argument a year from now.
5. **Revisit when something changes**: someone moves out, the rent goes up, or a couple moves in.
